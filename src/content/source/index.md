# Mumbrane Labs
## Field-based intelligence

Mumbrane is an independent research lab investigating field-based intelligence. We study how retained knowledge, reusable skills, and explicit constraints can support more general reasoning.

[Moth](https://mumbrane.ai/moth) is our experimental reasoning model: a practical system for testing how definitions and relationships can support checked answers.

## Define the world a decision should follow

Supply facts, define your criteria, and compile supported English sources into a reusable field—the retained knowledge and operations Moth can work with. Ask whether an entity meets a definition, then inspect the checked English or JSON result.

For example, define purchase readiness through funding and supplier requirements. Moth can establish whether an order meets those requirements, or report that the field lacks supporting evidence. Preview 004 provides a local CLI workflow with versioned fields and retained replay.

## Can knowledge compound into more general reasoning?

A definition can build on another definition. An interpretation skill can make a new sentence form usable. We study when these retained dependencies extend what a system can do on new tasks.

Our wider research explores layered fields and energy objectives as ways to organize knowledge and guide reasoning. Moth’s current constraint-based execution gives us a concrete starting point for that investigation.

- **Representation:** How should knowledge and its relationships be retained?
- **Composition:** When does new knowledge make earlier knowledge more useful?
- **Inference:** How should a goal shape the search for an answer?
- **Evaluation:** What evidence would show that the approach works?

Progress means preserving meaning, using dependencies correctly, and improving results on new cases—not merely storing more information.

## What limits intelligence?

Our long-term question is whether the physical conditions of our universe define an attainable frontier for intelligence. We are investigating how information, resources, and causality—including ideas from relativity—might help make that question precise.
